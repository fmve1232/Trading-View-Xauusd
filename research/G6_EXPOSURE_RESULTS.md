Generated by `audit/tools/g6_doc.py` from the result, evidence and check files listed at the end; `--check` confirms this file matches them. Registration: `research/registrations/G6-EXPOSURE.json` (committed before any code or run) and its amendment A1 (committed before any A1 code or run, and before any primary result was read). Code: `quantum/research/g6_exposure.py`. Tests: `tests/test_research_g6_exposure.py`.

**Decision: NONE.** Research in shadow mode. No limit is enforced anywhere, and nothing is proposed for production or the alerts. The operator's own rule (one MT5 trade open at a time) belongs to the operator, not to this study. The 1-open / 3-max proposal remains a hypothesis whatever these figures show.

## What the numbers are

- **Positions.** Closed trades of the frozen engine's arms. A position is open from its fill (the signal bar's close) to the close of its exit bar: exact for a FLIP, the latest possible instant for an intrabar stop or target. Concurrency is therefore never understated.
- **R.** Each position carries 1 R, its own stop distance. Net R is after the engine's cost of record, which is MODELLED: G2 broker costs are unverified.
- **The $650 figures** are MODELLED. They assume every position were taken at the operator's $650 planned loss. The operator takes one trade at a time, so they show what the alert stream would put at risk, not what anyone risked.
- **Policies** are replayed first-order. A blocked trade never opens and the arms are not re-simulated. Ties go to the higher timeframe, then to the registered arm order.

- **Two families.** PRIMARY is the registration as written (treatment and H4, 5m-1h). A1 is the amendment (every live arm, 5m-4h). Each family has its own 10 tests.

## Limits

- The replay is first-order. In reality, an arm whose trade was blocked could have taken a different later trade; that is not simulated.
- Ending each position at its exit-bar close overstates overlap by up to one bar on intrabar exits.
- Prices are Twelve Data spot mid. There is no bid / ask, so spreads, costs and slippage are the engine's MODELLED cost of record.
- Development engine runs have no external series (no futures, dollar, yields, equities, silver, COT, volume): the engine's macro terms are inactive there, so the development trades are the frozen engine without its macro inputs, not a replay of what production would have done.
- The forward window has entries on 11 New York trading days. Its figures describe that window only.
